# Authored typography and bilingual delivery

Read this for expressive MV/PV lettering, another-language picture versions, connected bilingual films or platform release packages. For story/action design, use [performance and compositing](music-mv-story-and-compositing.md). This reference describes craft and checks; it does not prescribe one visual style or authorize publishing.

## Words designed for the scene

Distinguish expressive headline/lyric artwork from clean captions and incidental text on props. Captions prioritize readable language access. A designed lyric may become an object, a punchline or a physical obstacle. Its visual treatment should express that purpose rather than merely decorate a font.

For energetic anime lettering, design the word silhouette, stroke weight, per-character or per-word placement, stress hierarchy and negative space. Faces/facets, controlled outlines, sharp shapes, paper/ink textures or graphic fragments can provide material and depth. Start from licensed fonts or authored glyphs, then compose the word as artwork. Changing the font or adding uniform extrusion alone did not meet the accepted example's standard.

Choose a small related vocabulary of treatments with different dramatic jobs, such as an interrupted phrase, a stamped verdict, paper carrying a sarcastic blessing, or a mechanical identity reveal. These are options, not required motifs. Fit the user's genre: cinematic live action may call for restrained tracked lettering rather than a manga burst. Avoid giving every line the same effect.

For each important phrase, define:

- **Meaning and hierarchy:** what the viewer should read first and what changes in the story.
- **Shape and envelope:** actual glyph bounds plus outlines, shards, shadows and overshoot; preserve room for the face, hands and decisive props.
- **Motion and contact:** anticipation, accent, readable settle, interaction and exit. Match the performed phrase or action, not only a nominal BPM.
- **Depth:** what passes in front/behind and why; an essential message cannot be accidentally obscured.

When replacing already approved text, preserve its timing, transforms, parenting and masks. Retain source text, style parameters, asset mapping and an editable generator or vector source alongside rendered artwork. Anchor the replacement to measured artwork bounds; font boxes and ornament bounds differ. Verify that motion/blur does not reveal clipping outside the planned envelope.

Watch in context at normal speed and phone size. Check the shortest display window, fastest transform, longest phrase and busiest background. A readable still does not establish readable animation. Sharp accents can work without repeated full-frame flashing or shaking that obscures the performance.

## One picture, two languages

Keep picture actions, shot identities and approved audio versions explicit. A language map can record source phrase, target meaning, sung timing, short on-screen wording and permitted line breaks. On-screen emphasis need not reproduce every sung word; retain full lyrics in clean captions where appropriate. Do not change a plot claim for a convenient rhyme or layout.

Measure each target phrase with the actual type assets. Try natural word groups and intentional line breaks before reducing size. Include decoration and animated overshoot in the envelope. English words often need a different composition from Chinese characters; squashing a literal translation into the original width usually loses both voice and readability. Unfamiliar split words and invented hyphenation need specific justification.

Reuse the accepted pictures when requested, replacing the intended language layers and matching captions. Inspect remaining text on signs, props and UI; classify intentional names/symbols separately from untranslated dialogue. Compare representative and crowded scenes side by side, including each language's latest corrected manifest. Sharing source footage does not prove that a text replacement preserved the final shot.

If language performances do not align, follow the actual phrase timings and section contacts. Preserve the approved master and adjust picture timing within the user's request; do not silently speed up an entire song. See [vocal production](../../yinzi-music-creation/references/vocal-production.md) for scansion and listening checks.

## Connected films and release cuts

For a continuous bilingual film, retain the last sung syllable, musical cadence and meaningful visual ending. Shorten a repeated intro only when that makes musical sense. Construct the transition from actual audio/video handles and verify picture, sound and subtitle timing together. A crossfade has no universal correct duration; do not hide a cut by fading both voices into silence. Review the boundary on both sides in the encoded result for black frames, frozen images, abrupt level changes and clipped tails.

Adapt a portrait cut around the story's face, full gesture, props and lyric hierarchy. Recompose or preserve a purposeful landscape stage when cropping would destroy these. Check target UI safe areas and the actual phone-sized result; a generic center crop can remove feet, bills or the crucial reveal. Short clips should still have a setup and payoff.

Package only the variants useful to the user's destinations: real video files, matching captions, covers, titles/descriptions and a preview/download entry. Keep source masters and editable projects intact. A manifest should distinguish language, aspect ratio, duration, version and local path so the correct file is easy to find. Use current platform requirements when available, without presenting an unverified upload limit as fact.

Credits can be playful while remaining accurate. Distinguish human experience/art direction/revisions, agent writing/planning/automation/editing, and tools that synthesize music or footage. This project's source is public and personal noncommercial use is free under its current license. That does not imply free hardware, commercial software, model services or unrestricted model rights. Preparing an addressed social draft does not send it.

## Rejected approaches and useful replacements

| Rejected result | Replacement that addresses the cause |
| --- | --- |
| A default font or identical extrusion everywhere | Author the word's silhouette, hierarchy, material and action; vary treatment by dramatic role. |
| Beautiful headline hides the character's decisive gesture | Recompose around story-critical space and inspect the complete animation envelope. |
| Long English phrase reduced until unreadable | Rewrite concise on-screen wording, use natural line breaks and measure a language-specific layout. |
| A strong pilot followed by repetitive filler | Apply the pilot's concrete quality floor to every section; review the weakest passage and fix its missing action or design. |
| Finished files exist only in chat or an unknown folder | Register a stable playable candidate at the milestone with its actual directory, link, version and remaining review work. |
| Hash/decode checks described as a successful emotional performance | Report technical validity, visual review, listening evidence and user approval separately. |

## Delivery evidence

Decode complete exports, verify stream properties and caption timing, and check actual playback including transitions and seeking. When preserving a master, compare decoded audio alignment and length against it; correlation supports preservation, not lyric or aesthetic correctness. Measure encoded true peaks where relevant rather than treating a mono downmix sample peak as stereo clipping.

Save the actual source/output identities, chosen manifests, narrow repair notes and unresolved findings. Update the artifact entry when a reviewed version supersedes a candidate. Local preview, delivered package, public website and social upload are different states; report only those actually verified. Do not repeat generation or encoding merely because later caption, metadata or packaging work failed if the completed media remains valid.
