# Performance-driven MV: story, rhythm and compositing

## Verified scope

A 9.17-second continuous-character sample with dynamic foreground extraction and native editable AE lyric/background layers was accepted as a useful direction. Earlier thickly painted room footage strung together did not read as an MV. The complete story MV remained unfinished when these lessons were written. Reuse the demonstrated technique without claiming a proven full film.

For ambitious story-led work, favor expressive performance, evolving visual ideas and deliberate lyric interaction. Respect the genre: holds and quiet passages may be intentional. Effects should advance understanding or emotion, not satisfy a quota.

## Design from the chosen music

Use actual master audio, sections, phrase accents and meaning. Beat analysis suggests candidates; a BPM written in a prompt is not the real drum phase. Listen and check contacts against the waveform; rounded tempo can drift across a long track.

Give important actions preparation before the accent, meaningful contact on it, and follow-through afterward. Apply this to walking, paying, handing over objects and reactions as well as dance. Keep motion continuous; use selective time remapping while retaining natural contacts. Global speed changes often make movement weightless.

For narrative MV, plot comes first and dance can release tension at suitable peaks. A shot plan records time range, story change, character/prop state, action accent, lyric interaction, assets and transition. Repeated phrases can change staging, scale, response or outcome; avoid repeating one flash/zoom/shake throughout.

## Separable material

Design stable costume and silhouette. A coherent chibi variant may suit emotional exaggeration. Request full action cycles and head/foot margins; cropped feet cannot be restored by editing. Choose a background separable from hair and clothes. Specific action text matters more than an elaborate room when compositing the character elsewhere.

Images establish identity/style, not a mandatory repeated pose. Describe initial state, motivated change and final state supporting the next shot. Check current image/video/audio reference support. Test a difficult representative shot before ordering many uncertain assets.

## Foreground and native AE

Use actual [foreground tools](foreground-segmentation.md) and [AE bridge](after-effects.md) contracts. The sample's uneven background needed adaptive treatment. Broad white removal punched through pale clothes; retaining only background-connected regions with a narrow threshold helped. These are input-dependent techniques, not universal presets. Compare segmentation, tracked masks or rotoscoping when the plate demands it; never invent executable parameter names.

Inspect alpha over black, white and saturated contrasting backgrounds through motion, including hair, fingers, boots and blur. Look for internal holes, spill, shimmer and shadows; one clean frame cannot validate a moving matte.

Keep editable text, characters, props, mattes, backgrounds and effects in AE. Lyrics can be pushed, caught, hidden behind, torn or reveal a new meaning. Design physical contact and occlusion so text has a relationship with performance. Keep key lines readable and faces/decisive hands visible. Test long English lines when sharing visuals with Chinese.

## Finish

Preserve chosen audio and use original PCM cuts when practical. Encoders may introduce delay/timestamp effects: compare decoded alignment against the reference. Correlation is useful for an unchanged track, not a universal sample-zero requirement for every codec or creative mix. Review sync at story contacts and musical transitions.

Watch the whole render normally, then inspect suspect cuts, fast motion and mattes. Check black/frozen tails, unfinished gestures, unreadable lyrics, accidental flashing, repetitive layouts, depth mistakes and fades that hide vocals. Keep editable project, timing receipts and the artistic verdict separate. A valid MP4 does not prove emotional delivery.
