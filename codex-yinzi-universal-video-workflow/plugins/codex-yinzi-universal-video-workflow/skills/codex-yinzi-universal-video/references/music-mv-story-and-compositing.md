# Performance-driven MV: story, rhythm and compositing

## Verified scope

A 9.17-second continuous-character sample established the performance/compositing route. It developed into Chinese and English story MVs of 191.958333 seconds each, plus a 375.416667-second continuous bilingual release. The user endorsed the high-impact chibi direction and later the authored anime lettering. The final files passed full decoding and recorded visual/playback checks; those checks do not imply a new complete listening review or final user approval of every frame.

Earlier thickly painted room footage strung together, slideshow-like staging and a lower-quality full-film assembly were rejected. The reusable outcome is a music-led, layered production with inspectable assets and editable timing, not a mandate to reuse this film's character, model, colors or visual genre.

For ambitious story-led work, favor expressive performance, evolving visual ideas and deliberate lyric interaction. Respect the genre: holds and quiet passages may be intentional. Effects should advance understanding or emotion, not satisfy a quota.

## Set the whole-film quality floor

Use the accepted representative sequence to identify concrete qualities: action continuity, emotional performance, readable composition, foreground edges, musical accents, integrated typography and a motivated visual reveal. Carry these through every section. A strong opening or chorus does not excuse filler elsewhere, and technical completion does not overrule an artistic rejection.

Plan a section-level contrast in staging, depth, palette, motion or lyric interaction. Repeated hooks can build recognition while their consequences evolve. For an energetic request, frequent new visual ideas may be appropriate; do not turn one user's requested cadence into a universal cut/effect timer. Nor does "show technical skill" mean using every available effect. Keep the viewer able to follow the action.

## Design from the chosen music

Use actual master audio, sections, phrase accents and meaning. Beat analysis suggests candidates; a BPM written in a prompt is not the real drum phase. Listen and check contacts against the waveform; rounded tempo can drift across a long track.

Give important actions preparation before the accent, meaningful contact on it, and follow-through afterward. Apply this to walking, paying, handing over objects and reactions as well as dance. Keep motion continuous; use selective time remapping while retaining natural contacts. Global speed changes often make movement weightless.

For narrative MV, plot comes first and dance can release tension at suitable peaks. A shot plan records time range, story change, incoming/outgoing pose and position, screen direction, eyeline, character/prop state, emotional intention, action accent, lyric interaction, assets and transition. Dialogue roles need identifiable speakers and visible reactions. Repeated phrases can change staging, scale, response or outcome; avoid repeating one flash/zoom/shake throughout.

Check cause before reaction: a bill enters before the character dodges; an object changes hands before its owner reacts. Put a narratively decisive stamp or message in the intended foreground so the actor does not hide the event. Retiming can align a contact but cannot create a missing gesture or repair an incoherent cause. Inspect the end of one shot beside the next shot's start; conceal a discontinuity only with an intentional motivated cut, not an unexplained teleport.

Keep an action-use record when reusing plates. Return to a motion only when its new context, crop, timing or consequence changes the meaning. A smiling shrug is not an adequate substitute for exasperation or sadness. If the available plate lacks the required emotion, use a supported alternative within the chosen style or revise the scene; do not fill the timeline with the same mismatched dance.

## Separable material

Design stable costume and silhouette. A coherent chibi variant may suit emotional exaggeration. Request full action cycles and head/foot margins; cropped feet cannot be restored by editing. Choose a background separable from hair and clothes. Specific action text matters more than an elaborate room when compositing the character elsewhere.

Images establish identity/style, not a mandatory repeated pose. Describe initial state, motivated change and final state supporting the next shot. Check current image/video/audio reference support. Test a difficult representative shot before ordering many uncertain assets.

Treat generated footage as material for the authored sequence. It need not contain the final room, every lyric, all props and the finished camera move. Plan character plates, scene elements and graphic interactions separately where that improves controllability. Keep accepted assets and user/model restrictions; a cheap but visibly weaker substitution is not a valid completion strategy. Reuse suitable material through a new meaningful composition before spending on a missing action.

## Foreground and native AE

Use actual [foreground tools](foreground-segmentation.md) and [AE bridge](after-effects.md) contracts. The sample's uneven background needed adaptive treatment. Broad white removal punched through pale clothes; retaining only background-connected regions with a narrow threshold helped. These are input-dependent techniques, not universal presets. Compare segmentation, tracked masks or rotoscoping when the plate demands it; never invent executable parameter names.

Inspect alpha over black, white and saturated contrasting backgrounds through motion, including hair, fingers, boots and blur. Look for internal holes, spill, shimmer and shadows; one clean frame cannot validate a moving matte.

Local hair-gap flicker was repaired by changing alpha only in the affected region while retaining the original RGB. An intermediate repair damaged a moving sleeve and was rejected. For similar repairs, scope the operation by space and time, compare original/repaired channels, and inspect the full range of nearby motion, including raised arms and crossings. Do not fill all transparent holes or erode the whole silhouette: valid gaps and pale clothing can disappear. Preserve rejected variants so a later revision does not reinstate them.

Keep editable text, characters, props, mattes, backgrounds and effects in AE. Lyrics can be pushed, caught, hidden behind, torn or reveal a new meaning. Design physical contact and occlusion so text has a relationship with performance. Keep key lines readable and faces/decisive hands visible. Test long English lines when sharing visuals with Chinese.

Use AE or a suitable layered compositor according to the user's environment. Keep native animation properties and asset lineage recoverable; do not flatten away the timing, parent hierarchy or occlusion merely to replace a title. For bold anime or expressive graphic work, read [authored typography and bilingual delivery](authored-typography-and-bilingual-delivery.md).

## Promotional keywords and scene-aware typography

For a short atmospheric trailer, use confirmed story terms or user-approved phrases. Do not invent a causal claim just to manufacture suspense. In one adaptation the user replaced an inaccurate “one banana” sentence with the keywords “电话微波炉 / 时空跳跃 / 凝胶香蕉”; that correction is specific to its scene, while the reusable lesson is to verify meaning before animating copy.

Treat words as part of the composition. Match the scene's light, color, empty space and depth; use restrained weight and spacing instead of default bold captions. A subtle focus reveal, a narrow light sweep, tracked movement or partial occlusion can connect lettering to the environment. Choose only the treatment that serves the shot, rather than stacking every effect. Let keywords arrive with their relevant image, settle long enough to read, and leave with the camera or transition. Keep faces, actions and important props clear. A plain subtitle slapped over the frame does not fulfill a request for integrated promotional typography.

Review at normal playback speed and at the intended phone viewing size. Check readability during motion, flicker/ghosting at dissolves, and consistency with existing successful titles. A contact sheet cannot validate a moving title's pacing. Keep the earlier render and change only the requested transition/text layer when the imagery and soundtrack are already approved.

## Finish

Preserve chosen audio and use original PCM cuts when practical. Encoders may introduce delay/timestamp effects: compare decoded alignment against the reference. Correlation is useful for an unchanged track, not a universal sample-zero requirement for every codec or creative mix. Review sync at story contacts and musical transitions.

Watch the whole render normally, then inspect suspect cuts, fast motion and mattes. Check black/frozen tails, unfinished gestures, unreadable lyrics, accidental flashing, repetitive layouts, depth mistakes and fades that hide vocals. Keep editable project, timing receipts and the artistic verdict separate. A valid MP4 does not prove emotional delivery.

Review the weakest sections against the accepted sample, not only the highlights. Record failures by time range and affected layer/action, then repair the narrow cause. Show stable playable candidates with their real project directory and review status at useful milestones; do not wait for a final delivery record before exposing a completed preview. Language/format variants and platform packaging follow the [delivery reference](authored-typography-and-bilingual-delivery.md).
